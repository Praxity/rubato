# Brief: scam text messages

From: a coordinator at a public library that runs free digital-skills workshops (fictional).

We run a one-hour in-person workshop for older adults on scam text messages, then send people home with something to practise with. Many of our learners have no internet at home and use a basic phone. Some read slowly or use large print.

Who: adults 65 and over, a wide range of reading levels. English, with a French version for one branch.

What we want them to do: when a text asks them to tap a link, call a number or pay something, stop and check with the sender through a number they already know before doing anything.

What gets in the way:
- Scam texts look official, use real company names and say something bad will happen soon.
- People feel embarrassed to say they aren't sure, so they guess.
- After the workshop there's no one to ask, and they forget the warning signs within a few weeks.

The workshop room has a projector and the library's laptops. We can print anything.
