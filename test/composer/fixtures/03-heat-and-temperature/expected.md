# Expected: heat and temperature

## Behaviour
When explaining an everyday observation about warm and cold objects, the student says which way heat moves and why, using conduction or insulation, without "cold" moving or objects "making" heat.

## Barriers and choices
| Barrier | Category | Expected choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cold as a substance; metal "is" colder; clothes make heat | knowledge (wrong ideas) | `knowledge-check` with contrast feedback, or `dont-know` | Surfaces each wrong idea and explains why it fails in this case |
| Writing an explanation with the right parts | skill | `self-check` | Students check their explanation against named parts; the teacher marks separately |
| "I did this in Grade 6" | motivation | `dont-know`, or a prediction question before teaching | Shows them what they don't know without a score |
| Wide reading range | design constraint | Short sentences, worked examples | Not a pattern choice |

Also fits: `worked-example`, `sort` (heat moves from or to), `scenario`, `flashcards`, `written-task` (the explanations the teacher marks).

Best fits that are planned: `first-answer` (explain the metal spoon at the start and again at the end; fallback `self-check` at the end), `write-distractors` (late in the unit, once students can name the wrong ideas; no fallback).

Doesn't fit: `check-first` and `test-out` (every student must do every part), `final-check` (the teacher marks; no recorded pass asked for).

## Must ask or flag
- French register for 13 and 14 year olds: *tu* or *vous*.
- How the teacher sees written explanations. Self-check answers stay on the device.
- Collect nothing beyond need: keep self-checks private.
- English and French versions of every lesson. Two 40-minute periods.
