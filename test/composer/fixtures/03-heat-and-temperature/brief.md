# Brief: heat and temperature

From: a Grade 8 science teacher at a French immersion school in Québec (fictional).

My students can recite "heat moves from hot to cold" and still say a metal bench is colder than a wooden one on the same morning, or that a blanket "makes heat". I want a short unit they work through on school Chromebooks in two class periods of 40 minutes.

Who: 13 and 14 year olds. The course must be in English and French, since half the class works in French.

What I want them to do: explain everyday observations (a metal spoon feels colder than a wooden one, a coat keeps you warm, ice melts faster on a metal plate) using heat transfer, not "cold" moving or objects "making" heat.

What gets in the way:
- Strong everyday ideas that feel true: cold as a substance that flows, metal "being" colder, clothes producing heat.
- Some students had this in Grade 6 and think they know it.
- The class has a wide reading range.

Every student must work through every part; I mark their written explanations myself. Don't collect anything about the students beyond what the course needs.
