# Brief: phone privacy refresher

From: the operations manager of a group of physiotherapy clinics (fictional).

Every year our front-desk staff take a 45-minute privacy course. They hate it. Most have done it five or more times and click through. Our privacy officer only needs a record that each person passed a final check, not that they saw every page.

Who: front-desk staff, mostly experienced, plus two or three new hires a year. They do the course at the front desk between patients, so it gets interrupted. English.

What we want them to do on the phone: confirm a caller's identity before sharing anything about a patient, and share nothing with a family member or employer unless the patient's file says they may.

What gets in the way:
- Experienced staff know the basics and are bored by them.
- The hard case is a worried family member, or an employer's HR person, who sounds official and in a hurry. People want to help and say too much.
- New hires don't know where the consent field is in the patient file.

We'd like the course to take experienced staff 15 minutes or less. The final check result goes to the privacy officer.
