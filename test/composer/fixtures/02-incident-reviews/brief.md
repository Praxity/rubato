# Brief: blameless incident reviews

From: an engineering manager at a mid-sized software company (fictional).

After an outage, our on-call engineers write a one-page incident review. Too many read like "Jordan pushed a bad config." We want reviews that describe what in the system let the failure happen and what we will change, without naming or blaming a person.

Who: software engineers, 1 to 10 years of experience, who have all written at least one review. They work at a laptop and would do this in one sitting of about 30 minutes. English.

What we want them to do: write the summary and contributing-factors sections of an incident review in blameless language, naming system causes and concrete follow-ups.

What I think gets in the way:
- Habit. The quickest sentence to write is "X did Y."
- They don't have a clear picture of what a good contributing-factors section contains.
- Some think blameless means vague, so they write "a change caused an issue" and stop.

We have three anonymised past reviews we can use as examples, one good and two weak. No scores needed; the team lead will read the first real review each person writes after the course.
